One building. Multiple filings. Several research questions.

EQUIPMENT VENDORS · RESEARCH

Read the kitchen scope, then identify the operator.

Verified public-record context

DOB filings B01415135-S1 and B01415135-S2, both dated October 8, 2026, describe work associated with the Sora By Masuta restaurant renovation. The publicly described scope covers kitchen equipment, interior partitions, lighting, finishes, and bathroom work.

These two filings belong to one job family. They should not be counted as two independent buyers.

Next action

Review both original filings and current permit status. Confirm the actual tenant, legal operator, timing and who controls purchasing before any supplier contact.

Not established: unplaced equipment order, vendor budget, or procurement window.

02
HOOD / FIRE VENDORS · VERIFY LINKAGE

Same building does not mean same customer.

Public-source context

A September 17, 2026 filing, B01353208-P1, is described in the public building-history mirror as installation of a commercial kitchen exhaust hood. This is a separate job family at the building.

Next action

Open the official filing, inspect the full scope, and establish whether the hood work belongs to the same restaurant and tenant. Do not automatically combine building-level activity into one opportunity.

Not established: a shared buyer, open contractor solicitation, or a new hood purchase.

03
DATA INTEGRITY · HOLD AUTO-MERGE

Verify the premise before naming the business.

Source reconciliation

PermitPlate's October 19 source-identity regression compared DOB filings B01415135-S1/S2 with DOHMH record CAMIS 50189702. The property tax-lot identifier matched, while the DOHMH building identifier used a placeholder.

Next action

Investigate the current venue and operator using original official records and building/tenant evidence. A property match is not proof of common business identity.

No automatic source-identity merge or live customer delivery is approved by this example.

Four steps before outreach

  1. Reopen the publisher data. Confirm that each filing still exists and review the latest status and scope.
  2. Resolve the business. Independently identify tenant, operator, premises, and buyer; do not infer one from an address alone.
  3. Qualify fit. Separate equipment, hood/fire, and unrelated building work for the subscribed vendor category.
  4. Authorize delivery. A real customer report requires founder review and explicit recipient/message approval. This public example has neither.

Evidence appendix

Official datasets identify the source records. The independent public building-history page supplies accessible descriptive context but is not the government publisher. Always reopen current primary records before relying on status.
