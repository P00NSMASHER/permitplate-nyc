A careful human process

Clear facts. Cautious conclusions.

PermitPlate finds useful changes in public records, checks the match, and explains why a vendor may want to research the project.

1. Read supported official sources

We begin with public administrative records, not scraped contact lists. The core sources are:

DOHMHRestaurant records and inspections DOB NOWBuilding job applications NYSLAPending liquor licenses

Not every source appears in every brief. We use only the records that add relevant, current context to the selected category and territory.

2. Check the match

Names and addresses in government data are often inconsistent. We compare the available identifiers, address, venue name, record details, and timing. Weak or unclear matches are left out.

Founder-reviewed means a person checks every delivered item. Automated tools may help collect and compare records, but the paid brief does not use or show a model score.

3. Explain the signal

Every delivered item includes:

  • the business or project name and address;
  • the relevant stage or record change;
  • a short “why it matters” note for the selected vendor category;
  • the source-update and PermitPlate review times; and
  • at least one direct official source link.

A weekly brief contains no more than 10 matching signals. If nothing credible qualifies, the member receives a no-matches note.

4. Keep dates honest

Public datasets contain several different dates. They do not mean the same thing.

  • Business event date is the official date attached to an inspection, application, or filing when the source provides one.
  • Source updated is when the publisher says its dataset changed. It is not an opening date.
  • PermitPlate reviewed is when the founder checked the item for the brief. It is not a filing date.
  • Product build describes the public website release and is tracked separately from source freshness.

The optional Starter Snapshot may include up to 10 still-active records first seen during the seven days before checkout. Those items are labeled and are not presented as post-subscription changes.

5. State the limits

PermitPlate is a research aid. A public record can be late, incomplete, corrected, duplicated, or superseded. It does not prove that a restaurant will open, that anyone plans to buy, or that a vendor will make a sale.

We do not promise exclusive leads, complete market coverage, daily volume, purchase intent, or opening probability. Members should verify the official record before outreach.
