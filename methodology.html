METHODOLOGY

How PermitPlate turns public records into useful sales signals.

We watch official sources, match records to the right venue, detect meaningful changes, and rank the result for the vendor category you sell.

1. Current sources

The launch product is NYC restaurant vendor intelligence. NYC DOHMH is the core restaurant source. PermitPlate may also use current/relevant New York State Liquor Authority pending-license records and NYC DOB NOW build/permit records when conservative identity/address and hospitality rules pass. Additional markets are not treated as production coverage until their source rights, freshness, matching precision and commercial usefulness are separately validated.

2. Matching records to the right venue

PermitPlate maintains a venue/project identity across source events instead of treating each public row as a separate lead. Business-name normalization, address normalization, source identifiers and source-specific evidence are preserved. Same-address evidence alone is not sufficient in food halls, malls, markets, office buildings and other multi-tenant locations. Ambiguous joins fail closed or remain review-only.

3. Keeping the source history

Each retained event should carry source system, source identifier, source date, observed-at timestamp, normalized entity keys, source URL where available, and a content/revision fingerprint when technically available. Corrections, amendments and later records do not erase the prior evidence; they create a new state transition.

4. Deciding what counts as a real change

PermitPlate compares the newest source state with the previously known entity state. A signal may be created or reopened by a genuinely new filing, lifecycle transition, corrected/superseding record, new corroborating source, materially stronger category-specific evidence, or another change that alters what a vendor should investigate. Duplicate rows, unchanged re-fetches and weak re-statements should not create a new opportunity.

5. Confidence comes before scoring

A commercially attractive record does not become deliverable if its identity match is unsafe. The model separates identity confidence, evidence strength and commercial priority. Low-confidence entity resolution cannot be rescued by a high vendor score.

6. Lifecycle stages

StageMeaning

7. Vendor-specific ranking

PermitPlate retains separate deterministic 0–100 prioritization scores for POS/payments, Insurance, Equipment, Hood/Fire, Waste, Pest, Linen and Distribution. Model V7 makes the reasoning explicit through four components:

The component model is a prioritization framework, not a probability model. Category-specific specialist scores require category-specific evidence; generic licensing or corroboration cannot by itself manufacture a Hood/Fire or Equipment opportunity.

8. What appears in a report

  • What Changed: the exact new or materially changed public evidence.
  • Why Now: the deterministic reason it deserves review for the selected vendor category.
  • Observed Facts: source-grounded facts only.
  • PermitPlate Inference: the model's interpretation, visibly separated from source facts.
  • Watch Next: the next public milestone worth monitoring.
  • Identity Confidence: confidence that records belong to the same real-world entity.
  • Evidence Strength: strength/currentness/specificity of the supporting public evidence.
  • Source lineage: IDs, dates and links for verification.

9. When an old opportunity comes back

An opportunity is not permanently “done.” A new permit, licensing change, pre-permit event, stronger corroboration, correction or later-stage public milestone can reopen a watched entity. Reopening must cite the new evidence and the prior state it changed.

10. Customer feedback

The preferred disposition vocabulary is Investigate, Watch, Irrelevant, and Already knew. These dispositions can tune customer-specific suppressions, category rules and territory preferences. They should not silently rewrite source facts or convert PermitPlate into an opaque purchase-intent model.

11. Definitions: new, opening, detected and freshness

New means first detected by PermitPlate after the subscriber baseline, or a later material change to an already known venue; it does not mean newly opened or necessarily newly filed. Opening or near-opening language is used only when cited public evidence supports that lifecycle stage. Detected means the first time PermitPlate observed the evidence during processing, not the source filing or event date. Source updated means an upstream provider dataset-metadata timestamp; it does not establish a filing date, opening date, or product-build date. Product build freshness is separate and is identified by the deployed build timestamp and source commit when available.

The normal feed begins at the subscriber's subscription baseline and is intended to contain post-baseline new or materially changed signals only. On first fulfillment, PermitPlate may separately label up to 10 still-active records first detected in the prior seven days as Starter Snapshot — active before you joined. Older backlog is never described as new.

12. Limitations

Public records may be delayed, incomplete, duplicated, changed, corrected or later superseded. Source freshness is monitored where technically available, but source freshness and product-build freshness are separate. DOHMH RECORD DATE, dataset extract timestamps, and dataset metadata update timestamps are never treated as filing dates or opening dates. A source change can reopen analysis; it does not automatically prove a commercial opportunity. Customers remain responsible for verifying official records and for lawful professional outreach.

13. Adding new markets

PermitPlate's architecture is intentionally reusable across permit-, license-, inspection- and public-change markets, but no city or vertical becomes a customer-facing product merely because data exists. New markets require source-rights review, a versioned schema, entity-resolution benchmark, material-change test corpus, false-join review and evidence that the resulting briefs create buyer value.
