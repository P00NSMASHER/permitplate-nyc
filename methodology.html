METHODOLOGY

Deterministic source-linked intelligence, not a black-box prediction.

PermitPlate uses official public records, conservative matching rules, longitudinal venue history, and vendor-specific prioritization.

1. Official sources

DOHMH is the core restaurant feed. PermitPlate also evaluates current/relevant New York State Liquor Authority pending-license records and NYC DOB NOW build/permit records. SLA or DOB evidence is counted only when the record is hospitality-relevant and passes strict identity/address rules. Not every signal is multi-source.

2. Venue identity and suppression

PermitPlate builds a durable venue graph rather than treating each public row as a new lead. Same-address evidence is not enough by itself in food halls, malls, markets, office buildings, and other multi-tenant sites. Building-level DOB work is excluded from venue-stage and score effects unless the filing can be safely tied to the operator/venue. Institutional and other nontraditional records can be suppressed from commercial delivery.

3. Lifecycle stages

StageMeaning
JUST FILEDEarly official applicant or safely matched source-first evidence without stronger buildout/health evidence.
BUILDOUT / LICENSINGCurrent licensing or venue-linked buildout/permit activity indicates a materially active opening process.
HEALTH PRE-PERMITAn actual CAMIS-matched DOHMH pre-permit Source Event exists.
MULTI-SOURCE NEAR-OPENINGMultiple current, safely matched sources support a later-stage opening signal. Multi-source status is never assumed for every venue.

Stage is evidence-driven. A raw global pre-permit count is never venue-level evidence; Stage 3 requires an actual CAMIS-matched Source Event.

4. Eight vendor-specific scores

PermitPlate calculates separate deterministic 0–100 prioritization scores for POS/payments, Insurance, Equipment, Hood/Fire, Waste, Pest, Linen, and Distribution. Scores combine stage, recency, commercial fit, corroboration, and category-relevant evidence. They are prioritization aids only—not purchase probabilities, purchase-intent predictions, opening probabilities, credit scores, or guarantees.

Specialist Equipment or Hood/Fire priority must be supported by category-specific physical/buildout evidence or explicit relevant hot-food evidence; generic licensing, corroboration, signage, or awning records alone cannot create specialist priority above generic timing categories.

5. What a useful signal may include

  • Why Now: the material public change that made the venue worth reviewing.
  • Purchase Window: a deterministic timing label based on the observed stage/evidence.
  • Watch Next: the next public milestone worth monitoring.
  • Confidence: a qualitative evidence-strength label, not a probability.
  • Source IDs and links: identifiers customers can use to verify the public evidence.
  • Observed Facts vs PermitPlate Inference: a visible separation between what the public source says and PermitPlate's deterministic interpretation.

6. First-customer and backlog semantics

The normal daily feed begins at the subscriber's subscription baseline. Normal delivery contains post-baseline new or materially changed signals only. On first fulfillment, PermitPlate may separately label up to 10 still-active records first detected in the prior seven days as Starter Snapshot — active before you joined; original detection dates remain visible. Older backlog is never described as new. If the baseline is ambiguous, customer fulfillment fails closed rather than guessing.

7. Delivery and CRM output

Production processing runs daily. A digest is delivered only when qualifying signals exist for an active subscriber profile. Nonempty digests are designed to include a CRM-ready CSV when technically supported, with the same selected signals, source lineage, and category ranking as the email. Delivery state is keyed deterministically to prevent duplicate treatment of the same customer/signal attempt.

8. Freshness and limitations

PermitPlate checks source freshness and preserves source timestamps. Public records may be delayed, incomplete, duplicated, changed, corrected, or later superseded. The product is a research and timing tool. Customers remain responsible for verifying official records and for lawful outreach.

9. Independence

PermitPlate is an independent commercial information service. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by New York City, New York State, DOHMH, DOB, or the State Liquor Authority.
